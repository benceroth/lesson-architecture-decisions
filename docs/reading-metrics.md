# Reading metrics

[DORA](https://dora.dev/) publishes research on software delivery performance. This repo borrows the idea of a few delivery and reliability measures, not DORA's full model.

| Measure | Why it matters here | RelayCart (illustrative) |
|---|---|---|
| Deployment frequency | Proxy for how cheaply the team can change the system. | 14 to 6 to 12 per week |
| MTTR | How long recovery takes; more layers usually mean longer diagnosis. | 24 to 71 to 31 minutes |
| Checkout P95 latency | User-visible effect of added hops. | 410 to 640 to 430 ms |
| Infrastructure cost | Execution cost only. | $18,000 to $47,000 to $29,000 a month |

Practical measures from the post to add: hours spent on upgrades, pages generated by each platform, lead time for a routine change, and how many teams it takes to diagnose a customer issue.

## Pitfalls

- **Correlation is not cause.** In RelayCart several changes landed together (Kubernetes, Kafka, mesh, GraphQL, 11 services, 35% more traffic). The numbers cannot say which one moved which measure. A bounded trial can.
- **Traffic growth confounds cost and latency.** Compare per unit of traffic where you can. RelayCart's traffic was 35% higher in both later states.
- **Averages hide tails.** Use P95 or P99 for latency; track MTTR distribution, not just the mean.
- **Small samples.** A few incidents make MTTR noisy. Say how many incidents were in the window.
- **Gaming.** Deployment frequency rises if you ship trivial changes. Pair it with failure rate or lead time.
- **Do not turn everything into dollars.** The post notes that converting every hour into a dollar figure implies precision the estimate does not have.
- **Measure before.** A baseline taken after the change is not a baseline.
